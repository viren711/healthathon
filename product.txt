DOCTOR KNOWLEDGE & PRODUCTIVITY ASSISTANT
=========================================

THE PROBLEM
-----------
Doctors lose time every day to three jobs that sit around clinical care:

  1. Looking things up. The answer is usually in a trusted textbook, but
     finding the right page in a 4,000-page book is slow. General chatbots
     are fast but may invent facts or cite sources that do not exist, which
     is unacceptable in medicine.
  2. Writing. Patient-education blogs, handouts and articles for colleagues
     are valuable but take hours to draft.
  3. Keeping up with research. Checking what the published literature says
     about a clinical question means searching papers and reading abstracts.

Patient and clinical information is also sensitive, so doctors hesitate to
paste it into online AI services.

THE SOLUTION
------------
One portal with three tools, running on an ordinary computer without a GPU.
Two of the three tools work fully offline.

1. Knowledge Assistant: cited answers from oncology textbooks
   - Ask a question in plain English and get an answer drawn only from two
     standard oncology references: DeVita's Cancer (11th ed.) and Gunderson &
     Tepper's Clinical Radiation Oncology.
   - Every sentence is cited to book, chapter and page, and the actual page
     opens in one click so the doctor can verify it.
   - When the books don't answer the question, it says "not available in the
     sources" instead of guessing. Three independent checks enforce this:
     a topic check, an evidence-strength check before the model runs, and a
     citation check afterwards.
   - "Find" mode lists every place a topic is discussed, page by page, and
     returns in about a second without using a language model.
   - Can be narrowed to 14 organ systems and 78 pathologies, e.g.
     Head & Neck -> Oral cavity & tongue.

2. Content Studio: article drafting
   - The doctor enters a topic and a short description of the piece. The
     studio asks a few questions (audience, tone, length, what to include or
     avoid, plus three written for that specific topic), then drafts the
     article.
   - Supports patient blogs, handouts, articles for clinicians and
     newsletters, in short, medium or long versions. Downloads as Markdown.
   - Safeguards: the model is told not to invent statistics or references,
     drafts are never cut off mid-sentence, and every draft is marked for the
     doctor's review.

3. Research Platform: evidence from the published literature
   - Research questions go to Scite (scite.ai), which returns relevant
     published papers with abstracts and supporting excerpts.
   - A local model summarises the evidence, citing each paper as [n], with
     authors, journal, year and DOI shown for each source.
   - This is the only tool that goes online, and the page says so.

Home page: a single launcher ("Ask the textbooks", "Draft an article",
"Search the literature") that opens the chosen tool with the task already
under way. It also shows each tool at a glance and the system's live status.

RESULTS (measured on a 6-core AMD Ryzen 5 5600G with no GPU)
------------------------------------------------------------
   - Retrieval found the right passages for 96% of labelled test questions
     (Recall@5).
   - Every off-topic and every unanswerable test question was correctly
     refused: 10/10 and 8/8.
   - On a set of 77 complex clinical case questions, 65 were answered with
     textbook references and 12 were marked "not in book". Results are in
     test/questions_results.xlsx.
   - Textbook answers take about 34 s, with text streaming from about 10 s.
     A 600-word article draft takes about 2-3 minutes.

TECH STACK
----------
   - Language: Python 3.12
   - User interface: Streamlit (multi-page portal with a top navigation bar)
   - Language models: open-weight Qwen models, 4-bit quantised (GGUF), served
     by llama.cpp on the CPU
       Qwen2.5-3B-Instruct  answering questions from the textbooks
       Qwen3.5-4B           article writing (Qwen3-4B available as a faster
                            option)
     A single model server keeps only one model in memory at a time and
     swaps between them on demand (about 4-10 s), so it runs in about 8 GB
     of RAM.
   - Retrieval: hybrid search combining FAISS vector search (BAAI
     bge-base-en-v1.5 embeddings) and BM25 keyword search, merged by
     reciprocal rank fusion, then reranked by a cross-encoder
     (ms-marco-MiniLM-L-6-v2)
   - Document processing: PyMuPDF for PDF text extraction and page rendering.
     A parallel pipeline turns 6,660 textbook pages into 15,844 searchable
     passages, each tagged with organ system and pathology.
   - External service: the Scite Assistant API, with Scite's literature
     search as a fallback
   - Testing: automated pipeline tests (65 checks, run against a stand-in
     model) and UI tests that click through the real pages with Streamlit
     AppTest (42 checks offline, plus live checks with the models running)

FUTURE SCOPE AND EXTENSIONS
---------------------------
   - More specialties: add textbooks for cardiology, internal medicine or
     paediatrics. The pipeline is not tied to oncology, and new books are
     indexed by re-running it.
   - Better handling of long case questions: rewrite a long case description
     into a focused search before retrieval, to reduce the few cases where
     the books do cover a question but it is refused.
   - Stronger fact-checking: add a model that checks whether each cited
     passage actually supports its sentence. Today the check confirms that a
     citation exists, not that it is correct.
   - Link the tools: let Content Studio drafts cite the textbooks and the
     literature, turning a draft into a referenced article.
   - Local literature library: save useful papers from the Research Platform
     into a searchable offline collection.
   - Multi-user deployment: run on a shared hospital server with sign-in and
     a GPU for faster answers.
   - Multilingual patient material: write or translate patient handouts into
     regional languages.
