1. its command line matches one of this project's own programs, **and**

Here's the pipeline in ten plain-English steps, start to finish:

**1. Start with the two books as PDFs.** Nothing is hand-curated — the system is given ~6,660 pages of *DeVita* and *Gunderson* and works out the rest itself.

**2. Read every page as text, in reading order.** Gunderson is two-column, so the pages must be read the way a human reads them; a naive extraction interleaves the columns and produces nonsense. This is spread across all CPU cores, taking about 50 seconds instead of 8 minutes.

**3. Clean each page and throw away what would poison search.** Remove the watermark, rejoin words broken across lines, strip superscript reference numbers, and discard the back-of-book index and bibliography pages entirely — those match any medical term constantly and would drown real content.

**4. Cut the text into sections that follow the book's own structure.** Chapter boundaries are read from each PDF's built-in table of contents, so every section knows exactly which chapter and page it came from. That trail is what later becomes a citation you can check.

**5. Split sections into passages of about 450 words, never crossing a section boundary.** Each passage is therefore about one thing, and its page reference is exact. Tables are kept whole with their caption, because a staging table split mid-row is useless.

**6. Convert every passage into a numerical fingerprint of its meaning, and separately index its exact words.** Meaning-based search finds passages that answer a question phrased differently; word-based search catches the things meaning blurs — drug names, stage codes like T2N0, specific doses. The system uses both.

**7. Label every passage with its organ system and disease.** Derived from the publishers' own chapter numbering, giving 78 pathologies across 14 systems. This is what lets you narrow the whole platform to "Head & Neck → Oral cavity & tongue".

**8. Tune the refusal thresholds against a set of labelled questions.** The system is shown questions the books answer, questions they don't, and questions that aren't medical at all, and the cut-offs are fitted to that evidence rather than guessed.

**9. When a question arrives, check it three times before trusting an answer.** First: is this even something these books could cover? Second: did the search actually find supporting passages, or is it reaching? Third, after the model writes: is every claim tied to a real source? The first two checks cost nothing — they refuse before the model is ever called.

**10. Show the answer with its sources, and let the reader verify.** Each fact carries a bracketed reference, each source names its book, chapter and page, and one click renders that actual PDF page on screen. If any check failed, the answer is discarded and the user is told it isn't in the books — never a guess.
