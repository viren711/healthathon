# Classes of cancer covered by the two indexed textbooks

A map of every malignancy the corpus covers, organised the way pathology
organises them: first by **tissue of origin** (what the tumour is made of),
then by **organ system** (where it arises).

Sources:

- **DeVita** — *DeVita, Hellman & Rosenberg's Cancer: Principles & Practice of
  Oncology*, 11th ed. Part V "Practice of Oncology", chapters 44–126.
- **Gunderson** — *Gunderson & Tepper's Clinical Radiation Oncology*, disease
  sections A–J, chapters 30–91.

Every entry below was taken from the PDFs' own embedded outlines (204 entries
in DeVita, 4,005 in Gunderson), not inferred from the text or from memory, so
the chapter titles and page numbers are the publishers'. Page numbers are
**PDF pages** — type them into the Find box or a PDF viewer's "go to page".

163 disease chapters in total: 101 in DeVita, 62 in Gunderson.

## Part 1 — By tissue of origin (the histogenetic classes)

This is the primary pathological axis. A tumour's class is decided by the
cell it arises from, which is why a "lung cancer" can be a carcinoma, a
neuroendocrine tumour or a sarcoma, and why those three behave differently.

### 1. Carcinomas — epithelial origin

By far the largest class, and the one both books spend most of their pages on.

## Part 3 — Cross-cutting categories

These are not organ systems but they are how a pathologist or oncologist
classifies a substantial number of real cases.

**Cancer of unknown primary** — DeVita ch. 108; Gunderson ch. 45 (head and
neck unknown primary). Classified by immunohistochemical profile rather than
by site.

**Metastatic disease by target organ** — DeVita Section 16 (pp 3392–3519):
116 brain · 117 lung · 118 liver · 119 bone · 120 malignant pleural and
pericardial effusions · 121 malignant ascites · 122 paraneoplastic syndromes.

**Other / rare** — DeVita Section 14 (pp 3261–3351): 108 unknown primary ·
109 benign and malignant mesothelioma · 110 peritoneal metastases and
peritoneal mesothelioma · 111 intraocular melanoma.

**Benign disease treated with radiation** — Gunderson ch. 76, and benign
brain tumours in ch. 33.

**Oncologic emergencies** — DeVita Section 15 (pp 3352–3391): superior vena
cava syndrome · increased intracranial pressure · spinal cord compression ·
metabolic emergencies.

## Notes, gaps and caveats

- **One boundary artifact.** DeVita ch. 65 "Molecular Biology of Kidney
  Cancer" begins on PDF p. 1815, ten pages before the outline marks Section 4
  (Genitourinary) as starting at p. 1825. It is listed above under
  genitourinary, where it belongs clinically, not under gastrointestinal
  where a naive page-range split puts it.

- **The two books are not symmetrical**, and the differences are systematic
  rather than random. DeVita pairs a *Molecular Biology of X* chapter with
  each clinical chapter; Gunderson does not, but gives far more subsite
  granularity in head and neck and CNS, and is the only one of the two with a
  dedicated paediatric section. For a question about molecular pathogenesis,
  expect DeVita; for radiotherapy technique at a specific subsite, expect
  Gunderson.

- **Endocrine and paediatric are single-source.** Endocrine tumours appear
  only in DeVita (except thyroid, which Gunderson covers under head and neck),
  and paediatric oncology only in Gunderson. Answers on those topics rest on
  one book, not two.

- **This lists what the books contain, not what the index retrieves well.**
  Coverage is not uniform: the corpus was built in full-book mode
  (`selection.mode: full`), so all of the above is indexed, but retrieval
  quality was only ever measured on tongue-cancer questions. Treat recall
  outside head and neck as unverified.

- **Classification schemes overlap on purpose.** A pancreatic neuroendocrine
  tumour is in the endocrine section and the neuroendocrine class; small cell
  lung cancer is thoracic by site and neuroendocrine by origin. Part 1 and
  Part 2 are two views of the same chapters, not two disjoint lists.
