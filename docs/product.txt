DOCTOR KNOWLEDGE & PRODUCTIVITY ASSISTANT
=========================================

A portal of six tools for oncology: cited answers from textbooks, treatment
recommendations from clinical guidelines, article drafting, literature search,
and a patient-facing assistant that speaks eleven Indian languages. It runs on
one ordinary computer with no GPU. Nothing is made up: every clinical statement
is quoted from, or cited to, a named source, and when the sources do not cover a
question the tool says so.

1. THE PROBLEM
--------------
Doctors lose time every day to jobs that sit around clinical care:

  - Looking things up. The answer is usually in a trusted textbook or guideline,
    but finding the right page in a 4,000-page book is slow. General chatbots
    are fast but may invent facts or cite sources that do not exist, which is
    unacceptable in medicine.
  - Deciding between options. Clinical guidelines hold the recommendation for a
    given case, but a lung cancer guideline alone has dozens of
    recommendations that depend on biomarkers, prior drugs and setting.
  - Writing. Patient-education blogs, handouts and articles for colleagues are
    valuable but take hours to draft.
  - Keeping up with research. Checking what the published literature says about a
    clinical question means searching papers and reading abstracts.

Patients and families have a different problem: reliable cancer information
exists (the NCCN Guidelines for Patients) but it is long, in English, and hard to
navigate, and general chatbots give confident answers with no source. Most
patients in India are more comfortable in their own language than in English,
and many prefer to speak rather than type.

Patient and clinical information is also sensitive, so doctors hesitate to paste
it into online AI services.


2. THE SOLUTION AT A GLANCE
---------------------------
One portal, six tools, opened from a home page. Four of the six work entirely on
the doctor's own computer. The language model is an open-weight Qwen model run on
the CPU, and it is NOT fine-tuned or trained on anything: all medical knowledge
comes from the indexed sources, and the model only writes the answer from the
passages it is handed.

  Home                 a launcher plus a short clickable card per tool
  Knowledge Assistant  cited answers from four oncology textbooks
  Content Studio       drafts articles and handouts from a short brief
  Research Platform    evidence from the published literature (online, Scite)
  Treatment Guidance   a case in, the matching ASCO recommendations out; falls
                       back to the textbooks when the guidelines have nothing
  Patient Portal       answers and reading material for patients, by voice or
                       text, in 11 languages


3. THE TOOLS, ONE BY ONE
------------------------

3.1  Home
   - A single launcher box: pick what you want to do (ask the textbooks, draft an
     article, search the literature, find treatment guidance, answer a patient
     question), type or tap an example, and land in that tool with the task
     already under way: the question already asked, the topic already filled in,
     or the case already read.
   - A short card per tool saying what it does and whether it runs on this
     computer or online. The whole card is the link into the tool.
   - Live status badges: whether the model server is up, which model is in
     memory, the size of the textbook and patient-guide indexes, the number of
     ASCO guidelines loaded, whether Scite and voice are configured.
   - The whole page fits on one screen of an ordinary laptop.

3.2  Knowledge Assistant: cited answers from oncology textbooks
   - Ask a question in plain English and get an answer drawn only from four
     standard oncology references: DeVita's Cancer (11th ed.), Gunderson &
     Tepper's Clinical Radiation Oncology, the MD Anderson Surgical Oncology
     Handbook and Oncologic Imaging: A Multidisciplinary Approach (8,685 pages,
     19,949 searchable passages).
   - Every sentence is cited to book, chapter and page, and the actual page opens
     in one click so the doctor can verify it.
   - When the books do not answer the question, it says "not available in the
     sources" instead of guessing (see section 6).
   - "Find" mode lists every place a topic is discussed, page by page, in about
     a second, with no language model involved, so it cannot invent a reference.
   - Can be narrowed to 14 organ systems and 78 pathologies, for example
     Head & Neck -> Oral cavity & tongue. The selectors sit above the question box.
   - Doctors can speak or type in English or Hindi. Hindi answers keep drug names,
     doses and staging terms in English, the way clinicians speak.

3.3  Content Studio: article drafting
   - The doctor enters a topic and a short description of the piece. The studio
     asks a few questions (audience, tone, length, what to include or avoid, plus
     three written for that specific topic), then drafts the article.
   - Patient blogs, handouts, articles for clinicians and newsletters, in short,
     medium or long versions. Downloads as Markdown.
   - It is deliberately NOT grounded in the textbooks and cannot cite anything;
     the page says so above every draft. The model is told not to invent
     statistics or references, drafts are never cut off mid-sentence, and every
     draft is marked for the doctor's review.

3.4  Research Platform: evidence from the published literature
   - Research questions go to Scite (scite.ai), which returns relevant published
     papers with abstracts and supporting excerpts.
   - A local model summarises the evidence, citing each paper as [n], with
     authors, journal, year and DOI shown for each source.
   - Two Scite routes: the Assistant API (written answer plus references) and,
     when that key is out of quota, Scite's literature search (papers only). A
     spare Scite key can be added and is used automatically.
   - This is the only clinical tool that sends the question text off the computer,
     and the page says so.

3.5  Treatment Guidance: a clinical case in, the matching ASCO recommendations out
   - Built on 19 ASCO clinical practice guidelines: lung (with and without driver
     alterations), breast, ovarian, myeloma, melanoma, thyroid,
     gastroesophageal, biliary, prostate, glioma, neuroendocrine, and
     cross-cutting guidance on ctDNA testing, growth factors, cancer in pregnancy
     and communication. 443 recommendations in all. The page lists the covered
     cancers so a doctor knows at once whether theirs is covered.
   - Start with a text box: type or paste the case and the question (or tap one
     of six checked examples), or simply tap the cancer. Rules read the cancer,
     setting, biomarkers, PD-L1, histology and prior drugs from the text and fill
     in the questions, which the doctor can change.
   - Then two or three optional questions, chosen from what that cancer's
     recommendations depend on. The best three recommendations appear at once and
     re-rank after every answer, with a bar showing how well each fits.
   - Each recommendation is shown word for word with its strength, evidence
     quality, guideline version, the reasons it matched, the trial statistics the
     guideline prints (figures in bold) and the PDF page. Contradictions exclude
     a recommendation (a HER2-negative regimen for a HER2-positive case).
     Nothing on the page is generated by a model.
   - When the guidelines have nothing (the text names a cancer none of the 19
     cover, or no recommendation matches), the page asks the oncology textbooks
     the same question through the Knowledge Assistant's pipeline. The answer
     appears under "From the textbooks" with a clear notice that it is NOT from
     the ASCO guidelines and where it does come from, its sources listed by book,
     chapter and page, and a button that opens the question in the Knowledge
     Assistant. If the textbooks do not cover it either, it says so.

3.6  Patient Portal: answers and reading material for patients and families
   - Built only on the 76 NCCN Guidelines for Patients: detection, prevention and
     risk reduction, treatment by cancer type, supportive care (distress, fatigue,
     nausea, palliative care, survivorship) and specific groups (young adults,
     older adults, caregivers). It uses a separate index from the textbooks, so a
     patient answer can never cite a textbook.
   - Ask: a question in everyday words ("I feel so tired") gets a plain-language
     answer from the guides, every sentence cited, with the guide page one click
     away. Everyday words are matched to the guides' terms for searching. It can
     be narrowed to one topic area or one guide. When the small model cannot
     produce a checked answer but retrieval found strong evidence, the guides'
     own top passages are shown, cited, instead of "not available".
   - Learn: each guide's own common questions, key points, questions to ask your
     doctor (downloadable as a checklist) and glossary, unchanged from NCCN, plus
     a search across all guides. No AI-written text, no model. A search or
     glossary lookup typed in another language is translated to English first, so
     it finds results.
   - Safety first, before any retrieval: crisis language gets a helpline message
     instead of an answer; urgent symptoms get a "contact your care team now"
     notice above the answer; "should I stop my treatment?" is answered in
     general terms with the decision left to the care team. The tool never tells
     a patient to start, stop or change a treatment. This works in every
     language: a crisis message written in Hindi is caught and answered with the
     helpline, in Hindi.


4. VOICE AND INDIAN LANGUAGES  (Patient Portal and Knowledge Assistant)
-----------------------------------------------------------------------
   - Speak or type the question. Patients can use any of 11 languages (English,
     Hindi, Bengali, Gujarati, Kannada, Malayalam, Marathi, Odia, Punjabi, Tamil,
     Telugu), picked at the top of the page, where a line saying "Choose your
     language" cycles through all 11 in their own scripts, or detected
     automatically. Doctors can use English or Hindi.
   - Both start in English, which needs no outside service. Typed English never
     calls Sarvam.
   - The question is translated to English and goes through the unchanged
     pipeline (safety screen, retrieval, the three refusal layers, the citation
     check). Only the already-checked English answer is translated back, so every
     sentence keeps its source reference in every language.
   - The answer comes back in the user's language, followed by the English. A
     Listen button reads either aloud, on request only. The Patient Portal's
     buttons and labels show English with the chosen language beneath; in
     English they show English only.
   - Recording: a red glow, pulsing dot and moving bars show when the microphone
     is recording, and "Recording sent" or "Recording cancelled" shows when it
     ends. A recording is capped at 30 seconds to save credits: the page sends it
     at the limit, and the server refuses anything longer without calling the
     service.
   - Built on Sarvam AI: speech to text (saaras:v4), translation
     (sarvam-translate:v1 for patients; mayura:v1 code-mixed for doctors),
     language identification, and speech output (bulbul:v4-flash, one voice for
     everyone, which reads all 11 languages).
   - Several Sarvam keys can be configured. They are tried in a fixed order, and
     a key that runs out of credit is skipped from then on, across restarts.


5. LOOK AND FEEL
----------------
   - Light by default, whatever the computer prefers. A sun/moon button at the top
     right switches to dark and remembers the choice.
   - A slow aurora of teal, cyan and indigo behind frosted-glass cards and a
     frosted top bar, gradient headings and a glowing gradient button for the
     main action. Motion is slow and switches off for people who ask for reduced
     motion. Text keeps at least 4.5:1 contrast, and every control shows a visible
     focus ring.
   - Each tool on the home page has its own colour. The Patient Portal is lighter
     and warmer (mint, sky and a touch of peach), because it is for patients.
   - Works on a phone and a tablet (checked at 390 px and 820 px wide on every
     page): nothing scrolls sideways, and the tools' cards stack. On a phone the
     navigation bar folds into a menu button, as Streamlit does by default.
   - No external fonts or images are loaded, so the look sends nothing anywhere.


6. HOW ANSWERS ARE KEPT HONEST
------------------------------
"Say when you don't know" cannot rest on a prompt, so for the textbook-based tools
it is enforced at three independent points, any of which can refuse:

   1. A topic check: is this a question these sources could answer? Cheap, no
      model call.
   2. An evidence check before generation: did anything retrieved actually clear
      the bar? It refuses before a model call is spent.
   3. A citation check afterwards: every sentence must cite a supplied source. An
      uncited or unverifiable answer is thrown away and the user sees the
      refusal, whatever the model wrote.

Retrieval is hybrid (FAISS vectors plus BM25 keywords, merged by reciprocal rank
fusion, then reranked by a cross-encoder). The model is told to write only from the
numbered passages. In Treatment Guidance nothing is generated at all: the
recommendation text is the guideline's own, checked word for word against its PDF.

What the citation check does NOT do: it confirms that a citation exists and points
at a supplied source, not that it is the right one. What carries the weight is the
evidence check plus the fact that the cited page is one click away.


7. PRIVACY: WHAT STAYS ON THE COMPUTER AND WHAT LEAVES IT
---------------------------------------------------------
   Stays on the computer
     - The textbooks, the NCCN and ASCO guides, the indexes, and the language
       models. Retrieval, answering, ranking and drafting all run locally.
     - Knowledge Assistant in English, Content Studio, Treatment Guidance and the
       Patient Portal in English: nothing is sent anywhere.
   Leaves the computer
     - Research Platform: the question and the conversation, to scite.ai, after a
       de-identification guardrail (rag/deid.py) has anonymised patient details (names,
       age, address, dates, IDs) in the question, the earlier turns and the answer
       that comes back. An age is generalised to a decade, not dropped. It is pattern-based: a bare
       name with no title or cue is not caught, so patient details should still
       not be typed in.
     - Voice and languages: a recording, or text in a language other than English,
       or English text that is being translated or read aloud, to Sarvam AI. A
       typed English question in English mode never leaves.
   Kept out of the repository
     - The source PDFs and the text extracted from them (copyrighted), the
       indexes, model weights, API keys and logs are all gitignored.


8. WHAT IT COSTS TO RUN
-----------------------
   - The clinical tools need no paid service. The only running costs are Scite
     (a plan with a monthly search limit) and Sarvam (pay per use, in rupees).
   - Sarvam prices used for the estimates: speech to text Rs 0.49 per minute,
     translation Rs 2 per 1,000 characters, speech output Rs 1 per 1,000
     characters, language identification Rs 0.35 per 1,000 characters.
   - Ways the credit use is kept low:
       every translation and audio clip is saved to disk and reused for everyone;
       the fixed messages (for example "I couldn't find an answer in the NCCN
       Guidelines") are translated and voiced once per language, then free;
       nothing is read aloud unless the Listen button is pressed;
       language is read from the script, so most typed questions need no
       language-identification call;
       a recording is capped at 30 seconds;
       a daily spending cap (Rs 500) refuses further calls until midnight, while
       cached results and English keep working;
       tests run against an offline stand-in and spend nothing.
   - A file, sarvam_api_usage.txt, is rewritten after every call with the total
     spent, the amount per service, and every transaction in detail (which key,
     what text, how many characters or seconds, what it cost). Spend is not shown
     in the web app.


9. RESULTS  (measured on a 6-core AMD Ryzen 5 5600G with no GPU)
-----------------------------------------------------------------
   - Textbook retrieval found the right passages for 96% of labelled test
     questions (Recall@5), and the right organ system for 38 of 38.
   - Every off-topic and every unanswerable test question was correctly refused:
     10/10 and 8/8.
   - On a set of 77 complex clinical case questions, 65 were answered with
     textbook references and 12 were marked "not in book" (the per-question sheet
     is test/questions_results.xlsx).
   - Patient Portal: the right guide page was retrieved for 35/35 labelled patient
     questions; every off-topic (8/8) and not-covered (6/6) question was refused,
     and all 12 safety test cases were routed correctly.
   - Treatment Guidance: 443 recommendations and 821 trial-statistic sentences
     parsed from 19 ASCO guidelines in about 1 second; all are verbatim in their
     PDFs. All 26 evaluation cases put the expected recommendation in the top 3
     when run through the page itself. On 10 new cases scored once, untouched, the
     right recommendation was in the top 3 for 5; that is the honest estimate for
     unseen cases.
   - Speed: textbook answers take about 34 s, with text streaming from about 10 s.
     A 600-word article draft takes 2 to 3 minutes. A textbook fallback from
     Treatment Guidance took about 3 minutes on a cold start (index and model
     loading); a warm one was not timed, but it is the same pipeline as a
     Knowledge Assistant answer.
   - Tests: 124 automated pipeline checks (against a stand-in model and a stand-in
     Sarvam) and 95 UI checks that click through the real pages offline, plus live
     checks with the models running.


10. TECH STACK
--------------
   - Language: Python 3.12
   - User interface: Streamlit, a multi-page portal with a top navigation bar and a
     shared stylesheet (app/ui_theme.py)
   - Language models: open-weight Qwen models, 4-bit quantised (GGUF), served by
     llama.cpp on the CPU, with no training or fine-tuning
       Qwen2.5-3B-Instruct  answering from the textbooks, the patient guides and
                            the literature summaries
       Qwen3.5-4B           article writing (Qwen3-4B available as a faster option)
     One model server keeps only one model in memory at a time and swaps on
     demand (about 4 to 10 s), so the whole system runs in about 8 GB of RAM.
   - Retrieval: FAISS vector search (BAAI bge-base-en-v1.5 embeddings) and BM25,
     merged by reciprocal rank fusion, reranked by a cross-encoder
     (ms-marco-MiniLM-L-6-v2)
   - Document processing: PyMuPDF for PDF text and page rendering. A parallel
     pipeline turns the textbooks into passages tagged with organ system and
     pathology, and parses the NCCN and ASCO PDFs into structured records.
   - External services (optional): Scite (Assistant API, with literature search as
     a fallback) and Sarvam AI (speech to text, translation, language
     identification, speech output)
   - Reliability: multi-key rotation for both services (rag/keyring.py), a
     single-flight lock so two identical requests never pay twice, and an on-disk
     cache shared by everyone
   - HTTPS: ./start makes a self-signed certificate for the machine and its LAN
     addresses, because browsers allow the microphone only on a secure page
   - Testing: scripts/smoke_test.py and scripts/ui_test.py (Streamlit AppTest)


11. SETUP AND OPERATION
-----------------------
   - Install: a Python 3.12 virtual environment and requirements.txt (the CPU build
     of PyTorch). Put the source PDFs in data/ and sources/ and run
     scripts/run_all.sh, which downloads the models and builds the corpus, chunks
     and indexes (about 90 minutes on a 6-core CPU, mostly embedding).
   - Run: ./start (model server and portal, https://localhost:8501 and the
     machine's LAN address), ./stop, ./start --http for plain http, --no-llm for
     retrieval only, --model 1.5b for a faster generator.
   - Keys: in .env (gitignored), copied from .env.example. Scite and Sarvam each
     accept spare keys.
   - Settings: config/ (models, thresholds and refusal messages, voice and the
     daily cap, the writer, the guides); the theme is .streamlit/config.toml.
   - The model server is a plain OpenAI-compatible endpoint, so LLM_BASE_URL in
     .env can point the platform at a different one.


12. SHARING IT WITH OTHER PEOPLE
--------------------------------
Everything runs on one machine. To reach it from elsewhere without moving it:
   - On the same network: the LAN address works once the firewall allows port 8501.
   - Over the internet from this machine: a free tunnel such as Tailscale Funnel
     (a permanent https address that reconnects after a restart) or an ngrok free
     static domain. The machine and the app must stay on.
   - Hosted free: Oracle Cloud's Always Free ARM server (4 CPUs, 24 GB) runs the
     whole stack; Hugging Face Spaces (16 GB, sleeps when idle) also fits.
     Streamlit Community Cloud and most small free tiers are too small for the
     local model.
Before making it public, note: the textbooks and NCCN guides are copyrighted, so
the indexes and source text should not be redistributed without permission, and the
app has no login, so anyone with the link can spend Sarvam credit up to the daily
cap.


13. LIMITS, STATED PLAINLY
--------------------------
   - Decision support and education only, not a clinical decision system and not
     medical advice. It carries whatever the editions and guideline versions say.
   - The citation check confirms that a citation exists, not that it is the right
     one (section 6).
   - Figures and images are not indexed; answers that would depend on reading a
     figure will abstain. Complex multi-column tables extract imperfectly.
   - Long case-vignette questions are sometimes refused by the evidence check
     even though the books cover them: the main known weakness. Every shortfall
     measured so far is a safe one (it said "not available" where it could have
     answered, never the reverse).
   - Treatment Guidance covers 11 cancers and 19 guidelines; for anything else it
     says so and falls back to the textbooks, whose answers are generated.
   - The Patient Portal's safety screen is a pattern match: it catches common
     phrasings of crisis and emergency, not every possible one.
   - On a 3B model on a CPU, answers take tens of seconds, and an article draft
     minutes.
   - Machine-translated answers are for understanding, not a substitute for a
     translator in a clinical conversation.
   - Hindi typed in Latin letters ("bukhar") is treated as English.
   - Retrieval quality was measured precisely only on tongue cancer (page-level
     ground truth); the other organ systems are measured at the coarser
     organ-system level.
   - The Scite Assistant API has a monthly search limit on its plan; the literature
     search fallback answers with papers only.


14. FUTURE SCOPE AND EXTENSIONS
-------------------------------
   - More specialties: add textbooks for cardiology, internal medicine or
     paediatrics. The pipeline is not tied to oncology, and new books are indexed
     by re-running it.
   - More guidelines in Treatment Guidance: the same parser takes any ASCO or
     similar guideline PDF; adding a cancer is a data change, not a code change.
   - Better handling of long case questions: rewrite a long case description into a
     focused search before retrieval, to reduce the few cases where the books do
     cover a question but it is refused.
   - Stronger fact-checking: add a model that checks whether each cited passage
     actually supports its sentence. Today the check confirms that a citation
     exists, not that it is correct.
   - Link the tools: let Content Studio drafts cite the textbooks and the
     literature, turning a draft into a referenced article.
   - Local literature library: save useful papers from the Research Platform into a
     searchable offline collection.
   - Multi-user deployment: a shared hospital server with sign-in, per-user limits
     and a GPU for faster answers, and a licence for the hosted source material.
   - Offline voice: on-device speech and translation models, so the Patient Portal
     in regional languages need not leave the computer.
   - Patient material in regional languages: reviewed translations of the handouts
     and the Learn section, rather than machine translation at read time.
   - An installable app for phones, with the microphone and a language picker on
     the first screen.
